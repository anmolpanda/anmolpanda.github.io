Bio

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher in the Management Information Systems Department at Purdue University's Daniels School of Business, where I work with Yu (Jeffrey) Hu. I received my Ph.D. in Information from the University of Michigan School of Information in 2026, advised by Libby Hemphill.

I am a computational social scientist. I study how digital platforms, news organizations, and AI systems shape what information people see, what they believe, and how they take part in civic and political life. A growing part of my work asks what happens when organizations deploy AI in production: whether people trust these systems, keep using them, and are willing to pay for what they produce.

Methodologically, I combine large-scale NLP (transformer models such as BERT and RoBERTa, and LLMs) with causal inference (staggered difference-in-differences, synthetic controls) and survey and experimental designs. My research has been published in PNAS Nexus, Scientific Data, Economic & Political Weekly, and at ICWSM, CSCW, and ASIS&T.

Research

Current Research

AI, trust, and local news. How do readers respond when newsrooms use AI to produce or repackage reporting? I study the staggered rollout of AI tools across local newspapers and run experiments that vary AI-disclosure labels and content depth, measuring real behavior such as reading time and subscription choices.

Generative AI for data curation. In an IMLS-funded project with ICPSR, we use LLMs to generate variable-level metadata for social science archives at scale (about 1.9 million variables), and test which generation strategies best support data search and harmonization (ASIS&T 2026).

Politicians' social media and local businesses. This project asks whether politicians' posts that mention a business, a place, or an issue change how customers behave, using millions of Google and Yelp reviews linked to US politicians' posts from 2016 to 2021. The design separates three channels: attention, emotional appeal, and issue salience.

Past Research

Digital paywalls and civic news. Using a 17-year panel of regional US newspapers, transformer-based topic classification, and a staggered difference-in-differences design, we showed that adopting a paywall reduces local news coverage, especially in larger cities (PNAS Nexus, 2025).

Social media engagement and political behavior. My dissertation used Cooperative Election Study data from 2016 to 2024 and an original survey to study how different ways of engaging with politics on social media relate to political attitudes and support for candidates, and how these relationships differ by race, with a focus on Asian Americans.

Framing in networked publics. We studied how motivational frames spread in the #HindusUnderAttack discourse on Indian Twitter, using RoBERTa models to classify frames at scale (ICWSM 2026).

Political communication and misinformation in India. At Microsoft Research India, I co-led NivaDuck, a machine learning pipeline that identified the Twitter accounts of tens of thousands of Indian and US politicians, and used the resulting data to study election campaigns, incivility, and COVID-19 misinformation (CSCW 2020, CSCW 2021, ICTD 2020).
